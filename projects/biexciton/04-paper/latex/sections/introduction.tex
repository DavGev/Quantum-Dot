\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Semiconductor quantum dots combine discrete, atom-like carrier spectra with
optical and electronic properties that can be tailored through composition,
size, and shape. This tunability supports applications in displays and
lighting, lasers, sensing, communications, energy conversion, bioimaging, and
quantum information \cite{garciadearquer2021}. In quantum-photonic
applications, individual dots can act as sources of single photons and photon
pairs \cite{senellart2017}. The radiative biexciton--exciton cascade produces
time-correlated photon pairs \cite{benson2000,moreau2001} and can generate
polarization-entangled photons \cite{akopian2006}. The biexciton binding
energy sets the spectral separation of the two cascade transitions, while the
exciton and biexciton radiative lifetimes set their emission time scales and
influence the attainable photon-generation rate.

The energy of a confined biexciton is not fixed by a single Coulomb scale.
Electron--hole attraction competes with electron--electron and hole--hole
repulsion, while the carrier masses and confinement geometry determine how
strongly the four particles can rearrange. Consequently, the sign and
magnitude of the binding energy can vary strongly with the material and dot
structure \cite{takagahara1989,bayer1998,juska2019}. This sensitivity makes
the biexciton a useful probe of few-particle correlations, but it also makes
its numerical evaluation demanding: the binding energy is a small difference
between two much larger correlated energies.

Strongly oblate dots are especially suitable for separating the roles of
axial and lateral confinement. Their two semiaxes provide independent
geometrical controls, while the large separation between axial and in-plane
energy scales permits an adiabatic treatment of the single-particle states.
Correlated few-particle states and radiative lifetimes have been studied in
strongly oblate full ellipsoids \cite{hayrapetyan2019}, including their
nonlinear optical response \cite{bleyan2022}. For semi-ellipsoidal dots,
previous calculations addressed electronic and excitonic states, binding and
recombination energies \cite{leon2005}, single-particle states and interband
absorption \cite{hayrapetyan2012}, and, more recently, perturbative exciton
binding, absorption, and photoluminescence in a hard-wall lens-shaped model
\cite{gevorgyan2026}. These semi-ellipsoidal studies do not treat the
correlated biexciton or its associated radiative lifetime.

The purpose of this work is to determine how the lateral semiaxis $a$ and the
axial semiaxis $c$ control the correlated exciton and biexciton energies,
biexciton binding, exciton coincidence overlap, and radiative lifetimes in a
strongly oblate semi-ellipsoidal GaAs quantum dot. We restrict the calculation
to the single-particle ground configuration and evaluate correlated
variational states using a state-matched importance transformation, adaptive
quasi-Monte Carlo integration, and a two-phase variational search. Two
intersecting geometry scans vary one semiaxis at a time to distinguish axial
and lateral trends. The idealized hard-wall effective-mass model is intended
to isolate those geometrical effects rather than reproduce a particular
fabricated dot. Accordingly, the principal results are the qualitative
dependences on dot geometry; the absolute numerical values should be
interpreted within the assumptions of the model. Particular attention is paid
to whether the small biexciton binding remains resolved at the available
numerical precision.
