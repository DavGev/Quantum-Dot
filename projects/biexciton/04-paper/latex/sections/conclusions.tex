\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We have calculated the ground exciton and biexciton states of a strongly
oblate semi-ellipsoidal GaAs quantum dot over intersecting axial and lateral
geometry scans. A ground-orbital-matched importance transformation, a full
global-rotation-fixed biexciton estimator, and the second-phase search with
two-budget verification were combined to control the numerical
sensitivity of the small binding energy.

Within the hard-wall effective-mass model, the main conclusions are
qualitative. The axial dimension controls the absolute confinement-energy
scale much more strongly than the lateral dimension. Enlarging the dot along
either direction weakens the biexciton binding, with the lateral scan
approaching the zero-binding threshold most closely. The central binding
estimate remains positive across the sampled range, but its sign is not
resolved at the widest lateral geometry.

Increasing either semiaxis also enhances the exciton coincidence overlap and
oscillator strength. This enhancement outweighs the reduction of optical
transition energy and shortens both calculated radiative lifetimes. The
lateral dependence remains pronounced across the sampled range, whereas the
axial dependence tends toward saturation.

The numerical values should therefore be interpreted as results of a
controlled model comparison rather than as absolute predictions for a
particular fabricated dot. Their principal contribution is to identify
distinct geometrical controls: the short axial dimension sets the dominant
confinement-energy scale, while lateral enlargement provides an effective
route for suppressing weak biexciton binding and enhancing radiative strength.
Extensions to finite barriers, dielectric mismatch, strain, and atomistic
confinement are needed for material-specific quantitative predictions. A
targeted higher-resolution calculation is also required to determine the sign
of the near-zero binding at the widest lateral geometry.
