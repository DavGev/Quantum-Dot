\begin{abstract}
We investigate the ground exciton and the biexciton state in a
hard-wall, strongly oblate semi-ellipsoidal GaAs quantum dot. Correlated
variational wave functions are evaluated using a ground-orbital-matched
importance transformation and adaptive quasi-Monte Carlo integration. To
avoid numerical bias from independently evaluated symmetry-reduced pieces, the
final biexciton estimator retains the complete symmetrized correlation factor,
all six Coulomb terms, and all four kinetic contributions, removing only the
redundant global azimuthal rotation. A safeguarded two-budget coordinate
pattern search is used to optimize the variational parameters at seven
representative geometries. Increasing the axial semiaxis from
$0.5\rB$ to $2\rB$ at fixed lateral semiaxis $5\rB$ lowers the corrected
biexciton energy from $89.65\,\Ry$ to $3.67\,\Ry$, whereas increasing
the lateral semiaxis from $3\rB$ to $10\rB$ at fixed axial semiaxis $\rB$
changes it only from $21.82\,\Ry$ to $20.09\,\Ry$. The central
biexciton binding energy is positive throughout the sampled range and
decreases monotonically as either dimension increases. Binding is resolved at
all geometries except $(a,c)=(10,1)\rB$, where
$\Ebind=0.031(20)\,\Ry$ is only marginally positive at the present
numerical resolution. Enlargement of the dot increases the exciton
coincidence overlap and oscillator strength, reducing the calculated exciton
lifetime from \SI{2.548}{ps} to \SI{1.437}{ps} along the axial scan and from
\SI{2.767}{ps} to \SI{1.068}{ps} along the lateral scan. Under the adopted
radiative-channel convention, $\tauXX=\tauX/4$. These results separate the
dominant axial control of the absolute confinement energy from the pronounced
lateral control of weak biexciton binding and radiative strength.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} semi-ellipsoidal quantum dot; exciton;
biexciton; binding energy; variational method; quasi-Monte Carlo integration;
radiative lifetime
